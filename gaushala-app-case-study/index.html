Gaushala App · Herd management with AI identification

Knowing every cow by her nose

A herd-management product for a gaushala (cow shelter). Staff register each animal, keep its health, milk and breeding records, and identify it later by scanning its muzzle, whose ridge pattern is as unique as a fingerprint.

The problem

Every record depends on knowing which animal you are looking at

A gaushala cares for cattle for their whole lives, including old, sick and rescued animals. Ear tags fall off or become unreadable, and many animals arrive with no history at all. When identification is shaky, health notes, vaccination dates and milk yields end up attached to the wrong cow, or to no cow.

The product had to make identification quick and reliable in a shed, and then make the daily records around each animal easy to log and easy to read back.

Who uses it

Caretakers and field staff

Work on a phone in the shed. They register animals, scan to identify them, and log health checks, milk yields and events such as vaccination or spraying.

Owner and manager

Work on the web dashboard. They see the whole herd, medical emergencies, feed stock and consumption, staff, and revenue or loss per gaushala.

How I approached research. The research combined two things: a competitive analysis of herd-management and similar apps, such as My Cattle Manager, and working assumptions about daily work in a gaushala. The analysis showed what records farms already track and where those apps fall short, mainly that they rely on ear tags and manual entry. The assumptions covered life in the shed: records kept in paper registers or from memory, tags that go missing, one phone shared by several staff, and weak signal. Together they shaped every design decision on this page, and the next round would validate them with caretakers.

How I built this map. Ramu is an assumption-based proto-persona, built from a competitive review of herd-management apps such as My Cattle Manager. It covers breeding, health, milk and finance records, works offline and lets a team share one farm account, but it relies on manual records and photos, with no biometric identification. That gap, plus the realities of a shared phone in a shed, shaped this journey. The next step would be to validate it with caretakers at the gaushala.

Mapping the domain

One cattle record, many kinds of data

Before drawing screens, I listed what a single animal's record has to hold. The list came out long and mixed: identity, lineage, biometrics, health history, milk and feed. Some fields are needed on day one, and others build up over the animal's life.

That split shaped the registration flow. It asks only for identity and media up front, then moves health and events into later steps and into the record itself.

Key flow · AI recognition

Designing around a model that can miss

Muzzle recognition uses a machine-learning model to match a photo of the nose against registered animals. A model like this fails in ordinary conditions: a wet nose, a moving head, poor light in the shed. So the flow gives staff a way through when the camera doesn't work.

Two ways to scan

A live camera scan, or a scan from a photo already on the phone when the animal won't stay still.

Tag ID as fallback

The result screen keeps a Tag ID field, so staff can confirm or find the animal by tag if the match is doubtful.

Show the evidence

The match shows the muzzle image next to breed, status and created date, so staff can check it's the right cow before logging anything.

Working with engineering

Prototypes in place of static specs. I built HTML prototypes of the main flows and walked the development team through them, so they could click through the scan, match and registration steps themselves. That answered most "what happens when…" questions before any code was written.

Shaping the data with the database engineers. I worked with the database engineers on the record structure and how data flows through it, so the model matched what the screens needed: the muzzle images captured at registration, and the health, milk, feed and event entries logged against each animal.

Iteration · My cattle

Three versions of the herd list

The herd list is the screen staff open most, so it went through three rounds in the file. Each round moved information closer to the row, so staff can act without opening every profile.

Version 1

Totals as two large tiles (115 total, 45 needing an update). Each row carries a coloured serial-number badge that flags a record needing an update.

Version 2

Totals move into the header to give the list more room. Each row now shows when it was last updated ("3 days ago"), so a stale record is visible without opening it.

Version 3

"Add new cattle" moves into the header, and tabs split All cattle from Events. Events is a dated checklist of herd-wide tasks such as spraying and vaccination.

Records and reports

One profile per animal, one report per question

Each animal's profile opens on its photo, serial number and muzzle video, with tabs for Information, Health, Milk and Events records. Health entries are grouped by date, with fertility stage, disease, vaccine and notes.

Reports answer herd-level questions: how many cows, heifers, bulls, steers, weaners and calves there are; how much milk the lactating cows gave this week, month or year; and how that splits between the dairy and the calves.

Admin web

The same record, built for a desk

Managers use a web dashboard with the same data model. Empty states on a new profile tell staff what to add next. Events and health checks open in a side drawer, so the profile stays in view. A full health history table records vitals (temperature, pulse, respiratory rate) next to medicine, supplements and feed.

Onboarding

Sign-in built for shared phones

Staff sign in with the gaushala's registration number, a one-time PIN and their phone number, then set a 4-digit PIN for daily use. That suits workers who share devices and may not have an email address.

Outcome

What shipped and what I'd do next

I delivered 50+ mobile screens and a set of admin web screens, covering onboarding, registration, recognition, records and six report types.

What I'd change in the recognition flow

Treat a scan as a suggestion, not an answer. Right now the result screen shows one animal and a "Start Now" button. If the model picks the wrong cow, the caretaker logs milk or a treatment against the wrong record, and nothing flags it. Next time I'd show a confidence state ("Strong match" or "Check the tag"), put the stored muzzle photo next to the new scan, and ask for one tap to confirm before anything is written to the record.

Make failure cheap. In a dim shed with a moving animal, the first scan will often fail, and the Tag ID fallback only appears on the result screen. I'd capture automatically once the muzzle is framed and sharp, give live hints such as "Move closer" or "Need more light", and after two failed tries offer Tag ID search on the same screen. Scans would also queue offline and sync when the network comes back.
